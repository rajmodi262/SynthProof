## Axis C — Benchmark and evaluation-methodology critique for DP synthetic tabular data; decisive question: is "scoring a marginal/clique-based DP synthesiser on a small fixed set of low-order statistics measures the mechanism's own statistic-selection rather than its fidelity" already published?

`12 sources · 7 kills · 3 candidates · saturation: True`

### Summary

VERDICT: THE CLAIM IS DEAD IN ITS STRONG FORM. The finding is published — three times, independently, and one of them is the AIM paper itself.

INFERENCE: I searched for the phenomenon under selection/workload/evaluation phrasings and then verified every hit by pulling full text (curl + PyMuPDF where WebFetch could not parse the PDF). Three papers state the result the project believes is novel:

(1) McKenna, Mullins, Sheldon & Miklau, "AIM", PVLDB 15(11) 2022 (arXiv:2201.12677), Section 5. AIM's own authors partition marginal queries into "supported" (selected during execution of AIM) and "unsupported" ("those not selected during execution of AIM"), give separate confidence bounds for each (Thm 3/Cor 1 vs Thm 4/Cor 2), plot measured true error split by that partition (Fig 2c, axes labelled "Unsupported Marginals" / "Supported Marginals"), and state the point of the exercise in the Discussion: "our uncertainty quantification guarantees enables analysts to understand which queries the synthetic data preserves well, and which it does not, which is important to know when performing downstream analyses on synthetic data." That sentence is SynthProof's methodological prescription, published by the mechanism's authors in 2022.

(2) Ganev, Xu & De Cristofaro, ACM CCS 2024 (arXiv:2305.10994v2), Section 5.3 / Figure 5. They break a pairwise fidelity metric down by whether the pair was selected by the mechanism — "Pairwise mutual information (connected/non-connected nodes extracted from the fitted networks for PrivBayes and MST)" — find "MST exhibits a more significant drop in scores of connected vs. non-connected nodes compared to PrivBayes", and attribute it to "the overfitting of the model to its objective function". That is the controlled selected-vs-unselected conditioning SynthProof claims as its cleanest contribution, published for MST/PrivBayes.

(3) Chen, Gong & Wang, arXiv:2511.13893 (17 Nov 2025), Section 6.3, Figures 6-7. They plot "ℓ1 distance error of selected and unselected marginals fitting by AIM and MargNet" ON ADULT and on Gauss10/Gauss30, and use the selected/unselected split to explain a cross-dataset ranking change: "on Gauss30, however, we observe that AIM can only afford a small proportion of marginals, leaving approximately 80% unselected. This results in its inferior performance compared to MargNet". This is the AIM-specific, per-marginal, selection-conditioned, cross-dataset version of SynthProof's H1 story.

Beyond those, the conceptual claim is textbook: Ponomareva et al., JAIR 86 Art. 17 (July 2026), state that "the relationship between a DP synthetic data algorithm's internal measurement strategy and a user-defined evaluation workload is the core of our taxonomy" and that "the workload-adaptive approach risks poor performance on 'off-workload' queries". And Hay et al. (DPBench, SIGMOD 2016) already published the ranking-inversion observation verbatim: "a data dependent algorithm A may have lower error than another algorithm B on one dataset, but the reverse may be true on another dataset."

THE SECOND, WORSE PROBLEM — A RIVAL EXPLANATION THAT INVERTS SYNTHPROOF'S OWN READING. Both AIM §5 and Chen et al. predict the OPPOSITE sign of SynthProof's Adult result. AIM's stated key insight is "marginal queries not selected have relatively low error compared to the marginal queries that were selected", and its Fig-2c commentary notes "the [error] of the non-selected marginal can be far below that of the selected marginal". Chen et al. find that on Adult specifically, AIM/PGM's unselected marginals are ALSO well estimated, and give the reason: "We hypothesize that this is caused by the sparse correlations in the Adult dataset. This sparsity ensures that most unselected marginals can be accurately approximated either by assuming independence or through conditional independence estimations."

INFERENCE: that is an alternative explanation for SynthProof's headline table. On Adult, AIM-on-unselected-pairs (0.0592) being statistically indistinguishable from the independent baseline (0.0609) is exactly what you would see if those unselected pairs are NEARLY INDEPENDENT IN THE REAL DATA — in which case the independent baseline is not a weak comparator on them, it is a correct model, and AIM has nothing to gain. That reading does not support "AIM's advantage exists only on pairs it selects"; it supports "AIM selected the only pair with strong dependence." SynthProof's own evidence is consistent with both, and the project has not reported the discriminating quantity. CONFIDENCE: high that this is unresolved in the current write-up, because §0.5 of REPORTS/01-LITERATURE-SURVEY.md reports only errors, never the real-data dependence strength of each pair.

MANDATORY FIX BEFORE VIVA (cheap, one afternoon): for every pair in the study, report the TRUE (non-private) mutual information or |correlation| in the real data alongside the two errors, and regress AIM's advantage on that. If AIM's advantage tracks true dependence strength rather than selection state, the confound claim collapses into "AIM selects the dependent pairs, which is the mechanism working correctly." If advantage tracks selection AFTER controlling for true dependence, the claim survives as a genuine (if now non-novel) confirmation. An examiner holding Chen et al. arXiv:2511.13893 can ask this in one sentence.

REPORTS/01-LITERATURE-SURVEY.md cites "Du & Li, arXiv:2504.14061 (2025)" at least three times (lines ~221, ~281, ~483, ~815). arXiv:2504.14061 is by Kai Chen, Xiaochen Li, Chen Gong, Ryan McKenna and Tianhao Wang (PACMMOD/SIGMOD 2025, doi 10.1145/3769764) — Ryan McKenna, an AIM author, is on it. "Du & Li" is a DIFFERENT paper: Yuntao Du & Ninghui Li, "Systematic Assessment of Tabular Data Synthesis", arXiv:2402.06806, CCS 2025. Two papers have been merged into one citation. Under the project's own standing rules this is exactly the failure mode being guarded against.

WHAT ACTUALLY SURVIVES (narrow, and it is an experimental-design contribution, not a discovery): the `fixed_workload` ablation in synthproof/generators/fixed_workload.py — AIM's engine with the exponential-mechanism selection deleted and a public, data-independent workload substituted at identical budget. AIM's own ablation (§6.3, Fig 2a/2b) swaps the selection CRITERION for MWEM's, it does not remove data-dependent selection; Chen et al. use random selection only to compare PGM-vs-NN fitting ability, not to price the selector's contribution to a benchmark score. Nobody I found runs "same engine, same ε, selection removed" as a control arm. That is defensible as a methods contribution and nothing more.

HOW TO SALVAGE THE THESIS CHAPTER: stop claiming discovery, claim replication-plus-extension. "Ganev et al. (CCS 2024) showed this for MST/PrivBayes's fitted graph; Chen et al. (2025) showed it for AIM's selected/unselected marginals across datasets of differing correlation density; we replicate it on a third axis — a budget-charged DP domain profiler whose output varies with ε, so the selected set itself moves with the privacy budget — and add a selection-deleted control arm the prior work lacks." That is honest, it is still a chapter, and it is survivable. Claiming the finding is new is not.

### Surviving candidates

**NARROW SURVIVOR: the selection-deleted ablation arm. Running AIM's own engine with the exponential-mechanism clique selection removed and a public, data-independent workload substituted at identical ε (synthproof/generators/fixed_workload.py) isolates how much of a benchmark score is attributable to the selector rather than to the estimator. No prior work I found runs that control.**

- *Why it might survive:* AIM's own ablation (§6.3, Fig 2a/2b) swaps the selection CRITERION for MWEM's and measures a 1.37x error factor — data-dependent selection is still present in both arms. Chen, Gong & Wang use randomly-selected marginals (Fig 6) but only to compare PGM-vs-NN fitting ability at a fixed selected set, not to price the selector's contribution to a benchmark score. Ganev et al. condition observationally on the fitted network and never delete the selection step. So 'same engine, same budget, selection removed' appears genuinely unrun. It is an experimental-design contribution, not a discovery, and it should be presented as exactly that.
- *Nearest prior work:* McKenna et al., AIM PVLDB 2022 §6.3 ablation (arXiv:2201.12677); Chen, Gong & Wang arXiv:2511.13893 Fig 6 (random-selection control)
- *Confidence:* med — I searched for selection-ablation under several phrasings and found none, but a negative from ~23 searches is weaker evidence than the positives above, and DP-synthesis ablations are often buried in appendices I did not read line by line
- *How to test:* Report, at matched ε and matched round count, the H1 metric for three arms: AIM (adaptive selection), fixed_workload (selection deleted, public random workload), and the independent baseline. Then report the fraction of AIM's advantage over the independent baseline that survives when selection is deleted. That number — 'X% of AIM's benchmark score on this statistic is the selector' — is the contribution. Cross-check the AIM appendix ablation table (Fig 2b) line by line first to confirm no arm there is equivalent.

**NARROW SURVIVOR: the ε-dependent selection channel. A budget-charged DP domain profiler unsuppresses categories as ε grows, inflating the domain, which changes which cliques AIM can afford, which changes which statistics it preserves — so the selected set is a function of the privacy budget, not only of the dataset.**

- *Why it might survive:* Chen, Gong & Wang vary the unselected fraction across datasets of differing dimensionality and correlation density; AIM's paper varies it implicitly through budget-awareness. Neither drives the selected set through a CHARGED PREPROCESSING STEP whose output moves with ε. The SynthProof observation that AIM selects AGEP x WKHP at exactly one of five ε values on ACS, and records its best ACS error there, is the visible signature of that channel.
- *Nearest prior work:* McKenna et al. AIM 2022 (budget-awareness: 'the number and size of the marginals selected... does not depend on the privacy budget available' is said of MST/MWEM/RAP/GEM, explicitly NOT of AIM); Chen, Gong & Wang arXiv:2511.13893 (unselected fraction varying by dataset); Hay et al. DPBench 2016 Principle 4 (domain-size diversity)
- *Confidence:* low-med — the components are all individually published and the composition is what is new; a hostile reviewer will call it a corollary, and DPBench Principle 4 plus AIM's budget-awareness taxonomy make that a fair charge
- *How to test:* Plot, per dataset, three curves against ε on one axis: profiler-induced domain size, number/size of cliques AIM affords, and the H1 metric. If the second tracks the first and the third tracks the second, the chain is demonstrated rather than asserted. Then show the chain BREAKS in the fixed_workload arm (no selection to perturb) — that is the falsification test and it is the strongest version of this result.

**POSSIBLE SURVIVOR, CONTINGENT ON ONE MISSING MEASUREMENT: that AIM's advantage tracks SELECTION STATE even after controlling for the true (non-private) dependence strength of the pair.**

- *Why it might survive:* If true, it refutes the Chen-Gong-Wang sparse-correlation explanation on Adult head-on and would be a real, citable correction to a very recent preprint. If false, H1 collapses to 'AIM selects the dependent pairs', which is the mechanism working as designed and is not a finding.
- *Nearest prior work:* Chen, Gong & Wang arXiv:2511.13893 §6.3 (sparse-correlation explanation for Adult); Ganev et al. CCS 2024 (Independent baseline preserving some correlation as a by-product of marginal fidelity)
- *Confidence:* low — this is currently UNTESTED in the project; I am naming it as the decisive experiment, not as a result. It could go either way and the team should assume it goes against them until measured.
- *How to test:* For every column pair in both datasets, compute the true non-private mutual information (or |Pearson r| for the continuous pairs) on the real data. Regress AIM's advantage-over-independent-baseline on (true dependence strength, selected/not-selected indicator, ε), with dataset fixed effects. Report the coefficient on the selection indicator with its CI. If it is not significantly non-zero once true dependence is in the model, say so in the thesis and downgrade H1 accordingly — the standing rules require that, and it is much better to have done it yourself than to have it done to you in a viva.

## Axis A — Deep generative models for DP synthetic tabular data (and whether the clique-selection confound is already published)

`19 sources · 4 kills · 2 candidates · saturation: True`

### Summary

VERDICT: the project's strongest surviving result is dead as a novelty claim. It is a correct rediscovery of a phenomenon that has a standard name, a published theory, a published controlled experiment using a deep generative model as the control, and a prescribed mitigation the project did not apply.

Q1 — Do marginal methods beat deep generative methods? YES, and it is settled consensus, not a finding. Four benchmarks read in full text agree: Tao et al. (arXiv:2112.09238, 2021), Ganev/Xu/De Cristofaro (ACM CCS 2024, DOI 10.1145/3658644.3690215), Castellon et al. DP-TBART (TPDP 2023, arXiv:2307.10430, Table 2 — AIM beats DP-TBART and four deep baselines), and Chen/Li/Gong/McKenna/Wang (SIGMOD 2025, PACMMOD 3(6) Art. 299, arXiv:2504.14061), verbatim: "statistical methods generally perform better than deep learning approaches", measured on ACSincome among others. GAN-based methods fall BELOW an independent-marginals baseline in Tao et al. — the same baseline SynthProof uses.

Q2 — Is the clique-selection confound published? YES. The kill is Chen/Gong/Wang, "Beyond One-Size-Fits-All" (MargNet), arXiv:2511.13893, 17 Nov 2025. Section 5 decomposes synthesizer error into Selected Marginal Error and Unselected Marginal Error with a bound on each. Section 6.3 asks verbatim: "Given an identical set of selected marginals, which model (NN or PGM) achieves higher fidelity when fitting those selected marginals and, crucially, when inferring the unselected marginals?" The answer reproduces SynthProof's result and diagnosis: PGM always wins on selected marginals, but on unselected marginals "the estimation error depends on the dataset's characteristics" — on Adult it wins because sparse correlations let unselected pairs be inferred by (conditional) independence; on dense data it loses; on Gauss30 AIM leaves "approximately 80% unselected. This results in its inferior performance." Three independent corroborations: Li/Zhang/Wang DPSyn (arXiv:2106.12949, 2021) — "which marginals to use has a large impact on the resulting score", with budget deliberately steered at the 3-of-98 attributes the scoring metric touched; Tao et al. 2021 — MWEM-PGM's strong score "may reflect the fact that its strategy is tuned to support classifier learning by favoring marginals that include the class label"; and Ponomareva et al., JAIR Vol. 86 Art. 17, July 2026 (arXiv:2512.03238), Remark 2, which names it "off-workload" error and prescribes the mitigation: "providing a broad workload (like all 3-way marginals)". Tab-PE (ICML 2026, arXiv:2606.08259) makes the benchmark-validity version of the argument and names Adult and Ding et al. 2021 (ACS) — SynthProof's two datasets — as the ones that flatter marginal methods: "much of the field has been implicitly optimized for this favorable setting."

Q3 — Non-marginal control? YES, three times. arXiv:2511.13893 Fig. 6 is precisely it (identical randomly-selected marginal set given to PGM and NN, error split selected/unselected). DP-TBART builds Dyck-20 and WikiText-103 as worst cases for marginal methods with a transformer as control. Tab-PE uses an XOR stress test.

Q4 — SOTA as of 2026: AIM and PrivMRF remain the utility leaders on standard low-dimensional tabular benchmarks (SIGMOD 2025), at a large runtime cost. Three 2025-26 works beat AIM on specific axes: Tab-PE (ICML 2026, +10% classification accuracy, 28x faster, targets high-order correlations, explicitly uses no foundation models); MargNet (arXiv:2511.13893, up to 26% fidelity-error reduction on densely correlated data, ~7x speedup); GEM+ (arXiv:2511.09672, ~10% over AIM at hundreds of columns — search-snippet-only, unverified).

A SECOND, SELF-INFLICTED PROBLEM the examiner will raise. SynthProof's H1 scored "structure" on the correlation error of ONE column pair. Every benchmark I read in full uses an all-pairs aggregate specifically to prevent this: Tao et al. average TVD "across all attribute pairs"; Chen et al. define Fidelity Error as TVD summed over all i<=j and Query Error as an expectation over all 3-way marginals; Ponomareva et al. prescribe a broad workload. So the confound is a predicted artifact of a single-pair metric the field abandoned before this project started. The finding is real and correctly diagnosed — but it is a demonstration of a known failure mode of a non-standard metric, not a discovery about benchmarks in general. Recommendation: recompute H1 with an all-pairs aggregate. If AIM's advantage survives, the project has a clean result; if it vanishes, that is the honest headline.

WHAT MIGHT SURVIVE (both thin, neither verified to the project's own saturation bar): (1) the epsilon-non-monotonicity of selection on a fixed pair — nobody varies epsilon while holding dataset and pair fixed, and "more budget made this reported statistic worse because selection changed" is mildly counter-intuitive; but it rests on three epsilon values and one pair and needs multi-seed replication before it can be stated at all. (2) The reframing that plays to the project's actual strength: propagate the mechanism's MEASUREMENT STRATEGY — the selected clique set — into the signed Privacy Data Sheet as machine-readable metadata, so a consumer can tell whether their query is on- or off-workload before trusting the data. JAX-IMPLEMENTABLE DP DEEP GENERATIVE MODEL (no PyTorch; local venv has jax 0.10.2, jaxlib 0.10.2, optax 0.2.8, numpy 2.4.6, dp_accounting; flax and torch absent). Two architectures, and the choice matters for what the project is trying to prove:

(a) GEM-style generator network (the easy path, but NOT a valid control). A small MLP maps noise to a distribution over the data domain and is trained to match noisy marginal measurements. Privacy comes entirely from the Gaussian mechanism on the marginals; the network trains on already-privatised statistics, so it is pure post-processing and needs NO DP-SGD, no per-example gradient clipping, and no new accountant integration — it charges to the project's existing dp_accounting exactly as AIM does. It is ~200 lines of jax + optax with no flax needed (hand-rolled params as a pytree). But it is still select-measure-generate, so it does NOT isolate the clique-selection confound. It is a scalability comparator, not a control.

(b) DP-SGD on raw data, DP-TBART-style (the harder path, and the only real control). A small autoregressive transformer or VAE over discretised columns, trained with DP-SGD (Abadi et al. 2016). This is the genuinely non-marginal mechanism, so it is the control the project's H1 actually needs. JAX is the better language for this, not the worse one: per-example gradients are `jax.vmap(jax.grad(loss))(params, batch)` — a first-class operation, whereas PyTorch needs Opacus's hooks. Clip-and-noise is then ~15 lines over the per-example gradient pytree, or use optax's DP aggregation. Accounting reuses dp_accounting's RDP/PLD subsampled-Gaussian accountant, which the project already delegates to. google-deepmind/jax_privacy (Apache 2.0) provides audited primitives for clipping, noise addition and batch construction plus DP-FTRL and matrix factorization — but its README defers dependency details to its docs site, and I did NOT verify whether it runs without Flax or Keras. Check that before adopting it; if it pulls Flax, hand-rolling clip+noise in optax is a defensible alternative and keeps the dependency surface honest for a project whose credibility rests on its ledger. Expect it to LOSE to AIM on Adult and ACSIncome — that is the point. A control that loses on-workload while being indifferent to which pair is scored is exactly the evidence needed, and its indifference is measurable: run both mechanisms over all column pairs and compare the variance of per-pair error, not the mean.

### Kills

**GENERALISED CLAIM UNDER TEST: 'any DP-synthesis benchmark scoring a marginal-based mechanism on a small fixed set of low-order statistics risks measuring clique selection rather than fidelity.'**

- Killed by: Chen, Gong & Wang, arXiv:2511.13893 (MargNet, 17 Nov 2025), Sections 5.1-5.2 and 6.3 — read in full text. The paper formally decomposes error into 'Selected Marginal Errors' and 'Unselected Marginal Errors', proves a bound on each, and runs the controlled experiment with the selected set held identical across a PGM and a neural network. It reports the cross-dataset ranking inversion and gives SynthProof's own diagnosis for it: on Adult, sparse correlations mean unselected pairs are cheap to infer, so AIM looks strong; on dense data the inference fails and the ordering flips; on Gauss30 AIM leaves ~80% of marginals unselected 'which results in its inferior performance'. Corroborated independently by three further sources: Li/Zhang/Wang arXiv:2106.12949 (2021) — 'which marginals to use has a large impact on the resulting score', with budget deliberately steered to the 3-of-98 attributes the scoring metric touches; Tao et al. arXiv:2112.09238 (2021) — MWEM-PGM's strong f1 'may reflect the fact that its strategy is tuned to support classifier learning by favoring marginals that include the class label'; and Ponomareva et al., JAIR Vol. 86 Art. 17 (July 2026), arXiv:2512.03238, Remark 2, which names the phenomenon 'off-workload' error and prescribes the mitigation ('providing a broad workload (like all 3-way marginals)').

### Surviving candidates

**Narrow survivor, low value: within a single dataset, AIM's clique selection for a fixed pair is NON-MONOTONE in epsilon, and the pair's error tracks selection rather than budget. SynthProof observes AGEP x WKHP on ACSIncome selected at only one of three epsilons, with error 0.0977 (not selected) -> 0.0395 (selected) -> 0.0626 (not selected).**

- *Why it might survive:* Every prior source I found varies the DATASET or the correlation density to expose selected/unselected asymmetry (MargNet: Adult vs Gauss10 vs Gauss30; Tab-PE: low-order vs high-order datasets). None varies EPSILON on a fixed dataset and fixed pair and shows the selection decision flipping back and forth, so that a per-pair error curve is non-monotone in the privacy budget. That is a distinct and slightly counter-intuitive observation: more budget can make a specific reported statistic worse, because the budget changed which cliques got chosen.
- *Nearest prior work:* arXiv:2511.13893 Section 6.3 (selected/unselected decomposition, but varied across datasets and across model class, not across epsilon on a fixed pair). Li/Zhang/Wang arXiv:2106.12949 Section 5.2 comes closest in spirit — 'The decisions often depend on the privacy parameter eps' and marginal engineering matters most 'when eps is small' — i.e. they already state that epsilon changes which marginals should be chosen, though they do not exhibit a non-monotone per-pair error curve. McKenna et al. arXiv:2201.12677 notes AIM 'knows to select larger marginals and run for more rounds when the [budget allows]'.
- *Confidence:* low-med — I found no publication exhibiting this specific non-monotonicity, but my search was targeted at the selection confound rather than at epsilon-sensitivity of AIM's selection, so absence of evidence is weak here. Also the observation rests on three epsilon values and one column pair, which is too thin to support a claim; it is a curiosity, not a result.
- *How to test:* Run AIM on ACSIncome across 8-10 epsilon values with >=5 seeds each, log the selected clique set at every epsilon and seed, and plot per-pair error against epsilon for all pairs, colouring by selection status. If selection is genuinely non-monotone in epsilon and error tracks it, the effect will show as a sawtooth in the per-pair curves and a smooth curve in the all-pairs average. Report the all-pairs average alongside, so the contrast between the confounded single-pair metric and the sound aggregate is the actual exhibit. If the sawtooth vanishes across seeds, the original three-point pattern was noise and should be retracted.

**Most promising reframing, and the only one that plays to the project's real strengths: the release artefact should DISCLOSE the mechanism's measurement strategy — the selected clique set — as signed, machine-readable metadata, so a downstream analyst can determine whether their own query is on-workload or off-workload before trusting the synthetic data for it.**

- *Why it might survive:* Ponomareva et al. (JAIR 2026, arXiv:2512.03238, Remark 1) establish that workload and measurements are distinct and that off-workload error is the central risk, and Remark 2 says the core design choice 'is not whether to take measurements, but how the measurement strategy is selected in relation to a potential workload'. But that framing is addressed to the ALGORITHM DESIGNER. Nobody in the sources I read proposes propagating the selected measurement set to the DATA CONSUMER as part of the release. This is a documentation/provenance contribution rather than a mechanism contribution — which is what SynthProof actually is, and it composes directly with the project's one surviving novelty claim (the validated Croissant integration): the selected cliques become a field in the Privacy Data Sheet, charged to the accountant and hash-chained in the ledger like everything else. It also reframes the H1 finding from 'we discovered a benchmark flaw' (dead) to 'we found a concrete case where an analyst could not have known their query was off-workload, and here is the artefact that fixes it' (defensible).
- *Nearest prior work:* Ponomareva et al., JAIR Vol. 86 Art. 17, July 2026 (arXiv:2512.03238), Remarks 1-2 — states the workload/measurement distinction and the off-workload risk but does not propose disclosure to consumers. Dibia et al. arXiv:2507.15997 (structured privacy labels — already killed as a standalone claim for this project) is the nearest artefact-side work and would need to be checked directly for whether it already carries a measurement-strategy field. ONS Methodology Working Paper Series No. 16 (Synthetic Data Pilot) discusses releasing metadata on how representative synthetic data is of the original — search-snippet-only, NOT fetched, and the specific proposal was not found there.
- *Confidence:* low-med — I ran only one search on this framing and it returned no direct hit, which is far short of the project's own saturation bar. Two things must happen before anyone relies on this: (1) fetch Dibia et al. 2507.15997 and check its schema for a measurement-strategy or workload field; (2) run a dedicated search pass on synthetic-data documentation standards (datasheets, model cards, Croissant RAI, ONS/UNECE SDC reporting guidance). Treat as an unverified lead, not a survivor.
- *How to test:* Two steps, in order. First, literature: fetch arXiv:2507.15997 and the Croissant RAI specification and grep both for any field expressing which statistics the generator measured; if either has one, this is dead too. Second, if it survives: add a `measurement_strategy` block to the existing Privacy Data Sheet emitting the selected clique list per mechanism per epsilon, charge nothing to the accountant for it (the cliques are already DP-selected, so publishing them is post-processing — but verify that claim against Ganev et al. arXiv:2504.08254 on domain extraction before asserting it), validate the extended sheet through MLCommons as the existing Croissant integration already does, and demonstrate the payoff with a worked example: an analyst query on an off-workload pair, flagged by the sheet, whose error the sheet correctly predicts to be large.

## Axis D — The 2026 frontier of empirical DP auditing: verification of arXiv 2604.18352, f-DP/GDP auditing, one-run auditing and its limits, audit sample efficiency, and whether "report the audit's operating range in the release artefact" (survivor S1) is published prior art.

### Summary

VERDICT ON THE PHANTOM CHECK: arXiv 2604.18352 IS REAL and the project is NOT citing a phantom. Confirmed by fetch and by reading all 7 pages of the PDF: Georgi Ganev (UCL/SAS), Meenatchi Sundaram Muthu Selva Annamalai (UCL), Bogdan Kulynych (Lausanne University Hospital), "Tight Auditing of Differential Privacy in MST and AIM", v1 20 Apr 2026, cs.CR, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2604.18352, Comments field verbatim: "Accepted to the 12th Theory and Practice of Differential Privacy Workshop (TPDP 2026)". Code at github.com/sassoftware/dpmm. Estimator is mu-GDP (Dong/Roth/Su) with Bayesian joint estimation after Zanella-Beguelin et al. ICML 2023.

BUT THE PROJECT ALMOST CERTAINLY MIS-DESCRIBES IT, AND THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT FINDING. It is NOT a one-run audit and NOT a drop-in estimator swap. Verbatim from Sec 3: they train "10,000 independent models (5,000 per D_out/D_in)"; the neighbouring pair is "D_out contains 10 identical records [0,0,0], and D_in adds one target record [1,1,1]"; each model "releases a synthetic dataset of size 50"; and they audit "a restricted configuration where only one-way marginals are measured. The dependency graph is fixed and no higher-order marginals are selected, so MST and AIM reduce to the same independent-marginal model." So: 10,000 runs, ONE target record, n=10 worst-case data, and AIM's adaptive clique selection DELIBERATELY SWITCHED OFF. Any thesis sentence of the form "Ganev et al. obtain tight one-run audits of AIM" is wrong on two counts and must be fixed before examination.

THE GIFT INSIDE IT — a citable diagnosis of SynthProof's 0.000. Sec 4 verbatim: "Notably, unstable threshold selection criteria (e.g., maximizing the estimated mu-hat on validation data) can select overly small thresholds, leading to excessive FPR and collapsing the empirical lower bound to zero. This explains why prior work reports eps_emp = 0 results in the strong-privacy regime [1, 24]." Their Fig. 3 ablation makes it concrete: Clopper-Pearson estimation gives mu_emp = 0.00 where their default gives 0.43. SynthProof's zero is therefore a KNOWN, EXPLAINED artefact of estimator and threshold choice, not a discovery. That kills any framing of the zero as a finding, and simultaneously hands the thesis a strong citation for why it happened.

Q1 — WHAT EXACTLY MUST CHANGE. Not the estimator alone; four coupled changes, and the estimator is the least important. (a) ADJACENCY is the dominant cause: SynthProof audits on real Adult/ACSIncome at n~45,000, where one canary moves a marginal count by 1 against Gaussian noise sigma. No estimator recovers signal at that SNR. Ganev et al. get tightness precisely because |D_out| = 10. (b) ATTACK: SynthProof's adversary is a nearest-neighbour similarity score over synthetic rows (synthproof/audit/canary.py:150, _similarity_scores) — a generic black-box data attack, exactly the "weak, poorly designed canary [leading to] a loose and uninformative empirical bound" that Ponomareva et al. JAIR 2026 Sec 7.5.4 warns about. Replace with MAMA-MIA (Golob et al., arXiv 2410.05506, SaTML 2025), which is purpose-built for marginals-based generators, or with Ganev's white-box noisy-marginal features. (c) ESTIMATOR: mu-GDP/f-DP + Bayesian credible interval (Zanella-Beguelin et al., arXiv 2206.05199, ICML 2023) instead of Clopper-Pearson on (eps,delta). (d) THRESHOLD selected on a held-out validation split and then frozen for test. If the project insists on staying one-run at m=60, the named paper is Mahloujifar, Melis & Chaudhuri, "Auditing f-Differential Privacy in One Run", arXiv 2410.22235, ICML 2025 — but Keinan, Shenfeld & Ligett (arXiv 2503.07199, v4 20 Feb 2026) characterise the maximum achievable efficacy of one-run auditing and name canary INTERFERENCE as the barrier, so I judge with high confidence that no one-run estimator rescues m=60 on 45k rows of real data.

Q2 — S1 IS BADLY DAMAGED BUT A NARROW VERSION SURVIVES. The PRINCIPLE is already normative in a NIST standard. NIST SP 800-226 Sec 2.7 verbatim: "Empirical approaches, including auditing, cannot prove that a system correctly provides a desired differential privacy guarantee" and "The results of auditing procedures can be difficult to interpret... which can significantly underestimate the privacy risk to outliers in the dataset and result in false confidence in the system's privacy protection." Reporting a RANGE rather than a point is also taken: Ganev et al.'s first listed contribution is a methodology that "reports the full FPR-FNR tradeoff... rather than relying on a single (eps,delta)", and Gomez et al. (arXiv 2503.10945, IEEE SaTML 2026 position track) argue exactly that a single (eps,delta) is "incomplete and potentially misleading". And the closest published recommendation to S1's spirit is JAIR 2026 Sec 7.5.4 verbatim: "it is recommended to audit with several sets of canaries to demonstrate robustness to a range of worst-case and realistic threats." What I could NOT find published, across twelve searches, is the specific instrument SynthProof has already built in synthproof/audit/detection_floor.py: calibrating the auditor against a generator with KNOWN, TUNABLE leakage to empirically measure the smallest m at which the audit fires, and then shipping that measured floor inside the signed artefact so a reported eps_audited = 0 is machine-readably scoped. Claim the positive-control measurement and its disclosure, never the principle.

Q3 — THERE IS NO STANDARD. Nothing mandates reporting empirical-privacy measurement uncertainty. NIST SP 800-226 imposes a reporting duty only on the PROVED side (Sec 2.5 verbatim: "When a variant is converted to (eps,delta)-differential privacy, the original privacy parameters should also be given (e.g., for zero-concentrated differential privacy, the value of rho)"). The live 2026 proposal, Gomez et al. SaTML 2026, is also proved-side only. On the empirical side the SoK by Annamalai et al. (arXiv 2506.16666v3) Sec 5.4 catalogues Clopper-Pearson, Katz-log and credible intervals and notes verbatim that "some do not report any confidence intervals at all" — i.e. the field itself has no convention. This is a genuine gap, but it is a small, reporting-hygiene gap, not a scientific result.

BONUS FOR AXIS C: Ganev et al. fix the dependency graph and disable higher-order selection so that "MST and AIM reduce to the same independent-marginal model." Specialists remove AIM's clique selection to get a clean measurement. That is independent, citable support for the clique-selection confound claim.

### Surviving candidates

**S1 (NARROWED, the only defensible form): An empirical DP auditor can be calibrated against a generator with known, tunable leakage to measure its DETECTION FLOOR — the smallest canary count m at which the audit reliably fires at a given leak fraction — and that measured floor can be carried inside the signed release artefact, so that a reported eps_audited = 0 becomes the bounded, machine-readable statement 'leakage, if present, is below what this instrument resolves at m = 60' rather than an unqualified null.**

- *Why it might survive:* Across twelve targeted searches I found the PRINCIPLE everywhere and the INSTRUMENT nowhere. NIST SP 800-226 Sec 2.7 warns that audit results 'can be difficult to interpret' and cause 'false confidence'; Aerni et al. (CCS 2024) show weak attacks underestimate leakage by an order of magnitude; JAIR 2026 Sec 7.5.4 says a weak canary yields 'a loose and uninformative empirical bound'. None of them proposes measuring how weak a given auditor actually is, and none proposes shipping that measurement with the release. The nearest published remedies are qualitatively different: JAIR recommends auditing with several canary SETS (varying the canary, not measuring the floor); Aerni et al. recommend comparison against a tuned DP-SGD baseline (a utility/leakage baseline, not an instrument-sensitivity curve); Ganev et al. report the full FPR-FNR curve (the audit's RESULT as a curve, not the audit's DETECTION LIMITS). The positive-control framing — inject known leakage, sweep m, record where the instrument fires — is standard in measurement science but I could not find it instantiated and disclosed for DP auditing. It is also already built and running in synthproof/audit/detection_floor.py, so it is demonstrable rather than aspirational.
- *Nearest prior work:* NIST SP 800-226 Sec 2.7 (March 2025) for the normative principle; Ponomareva et al. JAIR 2026 Vol 86 Art 17 Sec 7.5.4 (arXiv:2512.03238) for 'weak canary -> loose and uninformative bound' and for 'audit with several sets of canaries'; Aerni, Zhang & Tramer CCS 2024 (arXiv:2404.17399) for weak-attack-driven false reassurance and the tuned-baseline remedy; Ganev et al. TPDP 2026 (arXiv:2604.18352) contribution 1 for reporting a full tradeoff curve instead of a point; Wang et al. CCS 2024 (arXiv:2411.16516) for black-box audits passing under an overstated guarantee.
- *Confidence:* medium — the survival rests on absence of evidence from twelve searches, which is weaker than a positive kill. The positive-control idea is old and obvious enough in other measurement fields that it may exist in a tool's documentation, a NIST CRC blog post, or a paper my queries did not reach. Claim it as a reporting/engineering contribution and never as a scientific discovery, and state the prior art above in the same paragraph.
- *How to test:* Run three adversarial checks before committing it to the thesis. (1) Search the NIST Privacy Collaborative Research Cycle (CRC) materials, the SmartNoise/OpenDP and TAPAS repositories, and the dpmm library docs for any existing 'auditor sensitivity', 'positive control' or 'known-leakage sanity check' facility — a tool feature would weaken the claim more than a paper would. **There is no standard, guideline, or accepted convention for reporting the measurement uncertainty of an EMPIRICAL privacy audit, in contrast to the proved side where NIST SP 800-226 imposes a disclosure duty and Gomez et al. (SaTML 2026) propose GDP mu as the reporting format.**

- *Why it might survive:* This is a documented negative rather than an invention, which makes it robust. NIST SP 800-226 mandates reporting the underlying variant parameters (e.g. rho) on the proved side but mandates nothing empirical. The Annamalai et al. SoK Sec 5.4 surveys audit practice and states verbatim that 'some do not report any confidence intervals at all', with the rest split across Clopper-Pearson, Katz-log and Bayesian credible intervals with no convention over which. Ganev et al. themselves flag that their Bayesian lower bound 'does not necessarily yield valid frequentist coverage'. So published audits report incommensurable uncertainty objects, and the standard that governs DP evaluation is silent on the point.
- *Nearest prior work:* NIST SP 800-226 Sec 2.5 and 2.7 (March 2025); Gomez et al., arXiv:2503.10945, IEEE SaTML 2026 position track; Kulynych & Honkela, arXiv:2606.09582; Zanella-Beguelin et al., arXiv:2206.05199, ICML 2023 (the method for producing the uncertainty, not a standard for reporting it); Annamalai et al. - *Confidence:* high that the gap is real and documentable from the sources above; medium that it is worth much. It is a survey observation, not a result, and it will only carry weight in the thesis as motivation for the Privacy Data Sheet's empirical section — never as a contribution in its own right.
- *How to test:* Check ISO/IEC 27559 (privacy-enhancing data de-identification framework) and any 2026 ENISA or UK ONS/SACRO guidance for an empirical-privacy reporting clause, since the project's Five Safes/SACRO work already touches that literature. **The methodological fix for SynthProof's eps_audited = 0.000 is not an estimator swap but a change of ADJACENCY and ATTACK: audit on a worst-case neighbouring pair (Ganev et al. use |D_out| = 10) with a marginal-structure-aware adversary (MAMA-MIA), because at n ~ 45,000 real rows a single canary perturbs a marginal count by 1 against calibrated Gaussian noise and no estimator can recover that signal.**

- *Why it might survive:* This is not a novelty claim and should not be presented as one — it is the correct engineering diagnosis, and stating it correctly is what protects the thesis from an examiner who knows this literature. It survives because it is directly supported by the primary source: Ganev et al. obtain mu_emp ~ 0.43 only under a 10-record worst-case construction with 10,000 runs, and their Fig. 3 ablation includes |D_out| size as a varied component. The estimator (Clopper-Pearson -> Bayesian) explains their 0.00 -> 0.43 jump, but that jump was measured in a regime where the signal already existed; SynthProof's regime has no signal to estimate.
- *Nearest prior work:* Ganev et al., arXiv:2604.18352 Sec 3 (worst-case construction, 10,000 runs) and Sec 4 (threshold/estimator ablation); Golob et al., arXiv:2410.05506, SaTML 2025 (MAMA-MIA); Keinan, Shenfeld & Ligett, arXiv:2503.07199 (interference bounds one-run efficacy); Annamalai, Ganev & De Cristofaro, USENIX Security 2024 (arXiv:2405.10994), the [1] whose eps_emp = 0 Ganev et al. explain.
- *Confidence:* high on the diagnosis, from full-text reading of the primary source plus direct inspection of synthproof/audit/canary.py where the adversary is a nearest-neighbour similarity score over synthetic rows. Medium on the prescription, since I have not verified that MAMA-MIA transfers to AIM specifically (Golob et al. evaluate MST, PrivBayes and Private-GSD; TAMIS extends to graphical models but I read only snippets).
- *How to test:* Cheapest decisive experiment: keep everything else fixed and shrink the audited dataset to a worst-case pair of ~10-50 records, then re-run the existing Steinke auditor. If eps_audited stays 0.000 there, the problem is the attack, not the adjacency, and MAMA-MIA becomes the priority. If it fires, the thesis has a clean, honest before/after and a citable explanation for both halves. Either outcome is publishable inside the thesis and neither requires 10,000 retrainings.

## Axis B — LLM/NLP approaches to tabular data synthesis, and DP for LLMs

`18 sources · 5 kills · 4 candidates · saturation: True`

### Summary

Three of the four questions come back negative for novelty, and the fourth returns a mostly-negative engineering verdict — but the axis also produced one source that threatens the project's headline result, which is the most important thing here.

THE URGENT FINDING, and it is not about LLMs. Tab-PE (arXiv:2606.08259, ICML 2026, Tran/Backurs/Lin/Reis/Xiong/Yekhanin) already publishes the general form of SynthProof's strongest surviving claim. §1: "most prior evaluations sidestep the challenge of high-order correlations. Datasets widely used in the literature appear to be dominated by low-order dependencies." §5.4: "many high-order joint distributions can share the same 1-way and 2-way marginals." It also demonstrates the AIM ranking inverting across dataset classes — structurally the same result. The GENERALISED claim as currently worded is dead. What survives is narrower and must be reframed: Tab-PE blames the DATASETS, SynthProof's evidence indicts the MECHANISM. The per-epsilon ACS pattern (0.0977 not-selected, 0.0395 selected, 0.0626 not-selected) is non-monotone in epsilon and therefore cannot be explained by a fixed dataset property — that non-monotonicity is now the load-bearing evidence and should be promoted to the front of the argument. The deliverable becomes a reportable statistic: the overlap between the evaluated statistics and the mechanism's realized selection set. The SIGMOD benchmark (arXiv:2504.14061, Chen/Li/Gong/McKenna/Wang), co-authored by AIM's own author, evaluates AIM extensively and never raises this — that gap is real, and it is what the claim should be aimed at.

Q1: Yes, LLM-based DP tabular synthesizers with genuine end-to-end epsilon exist — DP-LLMTGen, DP-2Stage, Tab-PE, and Swanberg et al., four to five works, one at ICML 2026. DP-SGD plus post-processing closure holds. They do NOT break DP in the way the question supposed; they break it in the same narrow way SynthProof already documented and already killed (domain, tokenizer, format and column names declared free — DP-LLMTGen states outright that "feature names, categorical names, and numerical ranges are not sensitive"). DP-2Stage's authors themselves flag that their -U variant computes statistics on private data.

Q2: Comprehensively documented. LevAtt (arXiv:2512.08875) is a no-box MIA on released synthetic tables, achieving AUC 0.97 with TPR@FPR=0 of 0.64 on top runs against LLaMA-3.3-70B, and is evaluated against GReaT among others. Bordt et al. (COLM 2024) show verbatim memorization of Iris, Wine, Titanic, OpenML Diabetes. Nothing left to contribute.

Q3 — THE HONEST ADVERSARIAL ANSWER: do not add an LLM mechanism as a contribution. It would not formally void the epsilon (the DP-SGD theorem survives), so the framing "a mechanism that voids epsilon" is not quite the right worry. The real problem is worse in a subtler way and specific to your datasets. Bordt et al. Table 2 shows GPT-class models have memorized Adult's feature names, feature values, feature distribution AND conditional distribution — precisely the information a marginal-based DP mechanism spends its entire budget estimating. The pretrained weights are a second, untracked release channel from the same population, and SynthProof's ledger, whose entire premise is that every data-touching operation is charged and recorded, would be structurally blind to it. LevAtt states it plainly: exposure of the weights to the target data before the private stage "undermines" the technical validity of the guarantee. Google's own team (Swanberg et al.) took the mitigation seriously enough to build their evaluation on a January-2024 NYC Taxi extract instead. And when DP is applied properly, LevAtt reports DP-2Stage defeats the attack but lands at ML AUC 0.50 — chance. That is the honest cost. Recommendation: if the faculty requirement must be met, add an LLM mechanism explicitly labelled as a REPRODUCTION of DP-LLMTGen/DP-2Stage, run the Bordt et al. memorization suite as a precondition, record the contamination result in the ledger as a declared uncharged disclosure, and state in the thesis why it is not counted as a contribution. That framing is more defensible than either omitting it or overselling it.

Q4: The DP-safe metadata route is real but is not novel. Croissant Baker (arXiv:2605.15079, May 2026) already generates mlcroissant-validated Croissant 1.1 and — notably — explicitly declines to use LLMs for structural extraction on reproducibility and byte-traceability grounds, which is an argument to adopt rather than contest. Adopt its discipline: anything an LLM writes goes in a non-load-bearing description field, never a structural or numeric field in a signed artifact. The one genuinely encouraging signal on the whole axis: that May-2026 paper contains no mention of differential privacy, privacy budgets, privacy accounting, or synthetic data anywhere. The privacy-accounting fields inside machine-readable dataset metadata remain unoccupied, which is consistent with the Croissant MVP still being the live claim.

Caveat that must not be buried: I could not read "How to DP-fy Your Data" (arXiv:2512.03238, Dec 2025, Ponomareva/Kairouz/McKenna/McMahan et al.) past its section headings — both fetch attempts truncated. It is a Google-led practitioner guide covering DP synthetic data, LLM generation, empirical privacy auditing (§7.5) and lineage tracking (§7.7), written by the authors of AIM and of the accounting library SynthProof delegates to. It is the single most likely source to have pre-empted the project's framing, and I cannot clear SynthProof against it. Someone should read §7 in full before submission.

### Surviving candidates

**NARROW SURVIVOR: The confound is mechanism-side, not dataset-side. Tab-PE attributes low-order-benchmark flattery to the DATASETS (they lack high-order structure). SynthProof's finding is different in kind: for a workload-adaptive mechanism, the score on a specific low-order statistic is largely determined by whether that statistic falls inside the mechanism's own privately-chosen selection set (~6 cliques). The selection set is a per-run, per-epsilon, per-dataset latent variable that no published benchmark reports. Concrete claim: any benchmark scoring an adaptive marginal-based mechanism on a small fixed set of low-order statistics MUST report the overlap between the evaluated statistics and the mechanism's realized selection set, or the number it reports is not fidelity.**

- *Why it might survive:* INFERENCE: the strongest DP-tabular benchmark in the literature (Chen/Li/Gong/McKenna/Wang, SIGMOD, arXiv:2504.14061) evaluates AIM extensively, is co-authored by AIM's own author, and does NOT raise this — I searched its text specifically and found no discussion of the mechanism being scored on the marginals it selected, and no AIM-vs-independent inversion. Swanberg et al. (arXiv:2502.06555) scores on a 2-way-marginal workload against MST/GEM/JAM-PGM, all of which optimize 2-way marginals, and likewise does not discuss the overlap. So the mechanism-side statement is unoccupied even though the dataset-side statement is not. SynthProof's evidence is also of a form nobody else has published: per-epsilon selection tracking on ACSIncome where error follows selection exactly (0.0977 not-selected -> 0.0395 selected -> 0.0626 not-selected), which is a within-mechanism, within-dataset control that rules out the dataset explanation Tab-PE offers.
- *Nearest prior work:* arXiv:2606.08259 (Tab-PE, ICML 2026) §1 and §5.4 — the dataset-side version of the same warning, already in print and ahead of you. Secondarily arXiv:2504.14061 (SIGMOD) as the benchmark the claim indicts, and vldb.org/pvldb/vol15/p2599-mckenna.pdf (AIM) which states workload-adaptivity as a design goal.
- *Confidence:* med — the claim is genuinely distinguishable from Tab-PE, but only if it is framed as a mechanism-side measurement-validity claim with the selection-overlap statistic as the deliverable. Framed as 'low-order benchmarks are misleading' it is dead on arrival against Tab-PE §1. INFERENCE: the non-monotone ACS pattern (selected at the middle epsilon only) is the load-bearing evidence, because a dataset-property explanation cannot produce it.
- *How to test:* Instrument AIM to emit its realized clique set per run per epsilon per seed (SynthProof already has the ledger to record it). Then, for a fixed pair, plot correlation error against a binary selected/not-selected indicator across many seeds and both datasets. If error separates on that indicator with dataset held constant, the confound is mechanism-side and Tab-PE's dataset explanation is insufficient. Also run the trivially falsifying control: score a pair AIM never selects on either dataset, and check whether AIM's advantage over the independent baseline vanishes.

**HONEST NEGATIVE ANSWER TO Q3 (report this as a finding, not a claim): adding an LLM mechanism would NOT formally void SynthProof's epsilon — DP-SGD's guarantee plus post-processing closure survives, as DP-LLMTGen and DP-2Stage both demonstrate — but on UCI Adult specifically it would produce a number the accountant cannot interpret, because GPT-class models have already memorized Adult's feature names, feature values, feature distribution AND conditional distribution, which is exactly the information a DP mechanism spends its entire budget estimating. - *Why it might survive:* This is not a novelty claim; it is the defensible engineering verdict the faculty request needs answering with. ## Axis E — Statistical disclosure control, output checking, and data-blind refusal

`12 sources · 4 kills · 1 candidates · saturation: True`

### Summary

THE 403 GAP IS CLOSED AND THE CLAIM IS DEAD. I obtained the primary Handbook in full (v2.0, July 2024, 100 pp, UK Data Service mirror) plus the DwB/Eurostat Guidelines in full — the axis no longer rests on one practice guide and the SACRO paper.

DECISIVE QUESTION — 'does any published SDC tool or framework perform an automated, data-blind (metadata-only) pre-check that REFUSES a release?' — ANSWER: YES. DataSHIELD. This is not an inference from an abstract; I read the implementation. In dsBase/R/glmDS2.R, nfilter.glm takes `num.p <- dimX[2]` (number of columns) and `num.N <- dimX[1]` (number of rows), tests `if(num.p > nfilter.glm*num.N)` (default 0.33), and returns a hard refusal: 'ERROR: Model has too many parameters, there is a possible risk of disclosure - please simplify model'. Two integers, both dimensions, zero cell values, zero humans. Its sibling nfilter.subset (default 3) blocks subset creation when the row count falls below the threshold — the same object as SynthProof's '200 rows is below the 500-row floor'. Peer-reviewed as Avraam et al., Bioinformatics Advances 5(1):vbaf046 (2025), which explicitly makes human checking an OPTIONAL addition to the automatic mechanism, i.e. the mirror image of SACRO.

So the distinction SynthProof draws is REAL BUT MIS-SCOPED. It is real against SACRO: ACRO does read cell values, and its own paper says a 'pass' status 'does not indicate that the tool has certified that an output is safe for publication', and that SACRO exists 'to assist human checkers rather than seeking to replace them'. It is real against OpenSAFELY, which has hard numeric rules (redact <= 7 patients, round to nearest 5) and still routes every one through a person. But 'real against SACRO' was never the same as 'unoccupied', and the project generalised from one comparator.

Three further independent kills. (1) The policy of content-blind refusal is published verbatim in the DwB guidelines §3.8: 'reject any output on the grounds of volume or quantity only, irrespective of its content', with §3.6 adding metadata-manifest-completeness rejection. (2) The Handbook v2.0 defines mechanical rules-based accept/reject as the INCUMBENT regime the field is arguing against — and SACRO's own abstract concedes 'current automated rules-based approaches' already exist. SynthProof's gate sits on the incumbent side of that sentence. (3) The pre-execution timing escape route is closed by US 11,481,729 B2 (Intertrust, priority 2011): the secure environment 'may optionally impose a minimum number of individuals or sequences that can be targeted as a cohort for a study', at request-submission time.

RECOMMENDATION: move Axis E from 'UNREFUTED, never novel' to REFUTED. Do not attempt an upgrade. The one residual — that SynthProof's refusal is computed from a CALLER-DECLARED manifest and so costs no privacy budget, whereas DataSHIELD's dimensions come from the real matrix — is recorded above at low confidence and I advise dropping it: it is engineering hygiene, it leans on the accounting point from the already-dead Ganev-et-al. budget-charged-profiling claim, and to an examiner who knows DataSHIELD it will read as a retreat rather than a result. The defensible thesis sentence is descriptive, not novel: 'the preflight gate implements, for a DP synthetic-data release, the content-blind volume rejection recommended in the DwB output-checking guidelines and the dimension-based disclosure traps already standard in DataSHIELD.'

- `CONFIDENCE: med-high — the patent passage was fetched and quoted verbatim; 'med' only because patent disclosure text is broader and vaguer than a working implementation, and I did not read the claims set.`

### Surviving candidates

**NARROW AND WEAK — 'A release gate whose refusal is computed from a DECLARED manifest (schema + row count supplied by the caller) rather than from the real data, so that the refusal itself touches no records and therefore charges zero privacy budget.'**

- *Why it might survive:* INFERENCE: this is the only crack left after DataSHIELD. DataSHIELD's nfilter.glm and nfilter.subset are data-blind as to VALUES but are computed at runtime from the real matrix's dimensions (dimX[1], dimX[2]), so the refusal is a function of the private data and is not free. DwB §3.8 rejection 'irrespective of content' is executed by a human. The Intertrust patent's cohort minimum is likewise derived from the real cohort. A refusal derived purely from a caller-supplied declaration is, strictly, a different object with a different accounting property.
- *Nearest prior work:* DataSHIELD nfilter.subset / nfilter.glm (Avraam et al. 2025, doi:10.1093/bioadv/vbaf046, and dsBase R/glmDS2.R); DwB Guidelines for Output Checking §3.6 and §3.8; US 11,481,729 B2 (priority 2011).
- *Confidence:* low — and I recommend AGAINST advancing it. Three reasons. (1) It is an engineering hygiene property, not a research contribution: 'we checked a number the user typed in before we spent budget' is not a mechanism. (2) It is a hair's breadth from the project's ALREADY-DEAD claim about budget-charged domain profiling (Ganev et al. Apr 2025), which established the accounting point this rests on — so it is arguably a resurrection of a dead claim in a new coat. (3) A viva examiner who has read DataSHIELD will experience the distinction 'ours reads a declared row count, theirs reads the real row count' as a retreat, not a result. My adversarial judgement is that this candidate should be recorded and then dropped.
- *How to test:* There is no experiment that rescues it — the question is definitional, not empirical. The only honest test is a citation test: state the claim in one sentence to a reader who knows DataSHIELD and see whether they call it a distinction or a dodge. INFERENCE: they will call it a dodge.

### Measurement convention — the ceiling is borrowed, and attributed

The audit ceiling reported beside every ε_audited is `log(r / ln(1/α))`, a one-line corollary
of Steinke, Nasr & Jagielski (NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2305.08846) Thm 2.1 — **not a result of
ours** — and the same quantity is already named *maximum auditable epsilon* by Annamalai,
Ganev & De Cristofaro (USENIX Sec 2024, arXiv:2405.10994) §2.2.

Reporting it alongside the measurement is a transfer of **limit-of-detection (LoD) reporting**
from analytical chemistry, where **MIQE 2.0** (Bustin et al., *Clinical Chemistry*
2025;71(6):634–651) mandates LoD/LLOQ disclosure and a laboratory reports *"Not Detected,
< LOD"* rather than zero. The transfer is the claim; the convention is not our invention.
