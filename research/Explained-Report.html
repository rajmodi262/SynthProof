1 · How to read these numbers

Five plain-English explainers — what each metric measures, what a good value looks like, and why it matters.
Correlation error

How faithfully the synthetic data keeps the relationship between two columns (e.g. age and income). We compare the correlation in the synthetic data to the correlation in the real data.

Good value: 0 = identical to real; below ~0.02 is near-perfect; above ~0.1 means the relationship is distorted.

So what: It tells you the synthetic data still has the real patterns an analyst would study.

TSTR F1 vs real-data ceiling

Train a model on the SYNTHETIC data, then test it on REAL data (TSTR). Divide by the score of a model trained on the real data (TRTR = the ceiling).

Good value: Close to 100% = the synthetic data is as useful as the real, private data for building models.

So what: It proves the synthetic data can replace the sensitive data for actual machine learning — the whole point.

Proved ε (epsilon)

The privacy budget mathematically charged for the release. Lower ε = stronger privacy.

Good value: Whatever you asked for or less — 'calibration never overspends' means we never charge more than requested.

So what: It is the guarantee itself: a smaller ε means an attacker learns less about any one person.

Audited ε / GDP μ

An empirical attack's estimate of how much actually leaked. The standard canary audit reads 0.000 — but that is the tool's DETECTION FLOOR, not proof of zero.

Good value: A number reported WITH its detection limit. Ours: canary=0 (floor), GDP μ≈0.15–0.36 (a real signal).

So what: Being honest that '0' means 'the instrument can't see leakage this small', not 'nothing leaked', is itself a contribution.

Membership-inference AUC

Can an attacker guess whether one specific person was in the training data? Measured as AUC.

Good value: 0.5 = a coin flip = safe. Ours stays within ~0.05 of 0.5 everywhere.

So what: It is the direct 'can you re-identify me?' test — and the answer is no better than chance.

5 · What we deliberately do NOT claim

  • Not a novel generator — AIM/MST are integrations of published methods; we reproduce their utility, we don't beat them.
  • Not "audited ε = 0 means no leakage" — that is the canary tool's detection floor; the honest reading is the operating range.
  • Not "15/15 parameters" and not "we recover the original data" — neither is true; we detect what a release leaks beyond its ε.
  • Full data helps correlation fidelity (Bank 32×, ACS 12×) but not uniformly at every ε — the DP domain grows with n.
  • Multi-table auditing ships; multi-table synthesis is declared future work.
