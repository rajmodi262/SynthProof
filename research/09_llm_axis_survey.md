## Summary

DIRECT ANSWERS TO THE FOUR QUESTIONS.

Q1 — Does an LLM-based DP tabular synthesizer with a formal end-to-end epsilon exist? YES, formally, and it has for two years. DP-LLMTGen (arXiv:2406.01457, Jun 2024) and DP-2Stage (arXiv:2412.02467, TMLR March 2025) both DP-SGD-fine-tune a pretrained LLM and inherit (eps,delta) by post-processing; DP-2Stage is evaluated at eps=1 and eps=8, delta=1e-5, on Adult, Airline and Texas Hospital Discharge. But the guarantee is contingent in two places, and BOTH are documented by the papers' own authors or by direct successors. (a) DP-2Stage states its guarantee holds only "assuming that the public dataset used is indeed 'public'", and then admits verbatim that "the proposed DP-2Stage-U method utilizes private data to compute statistics for the uniformly sampled dataset, which weakens the stated privacy guarantees" — column ranges and category sets flow into un-budgeted stage-1 training. That is precisely the un-noised domain-profiling leak SynthProof's own adversarial audit already found and fixed. (b) SynBench (arXiv:2509.14594) shows empirically that on contaminated data, "observed privacy loss can exceed the bounds implied by claimed (eps,delta)-DP configurations" because "access to private data during pre-training is not accounted for in the privacy guarantees". So: formally yes, operationally conditional, and the conditions fail exactly where SynthProof would deploy it.

Q2 — Documented failure mode where LLM tabular synthesis leaks memorised rows? YES, thoroughly, three ways. "When Tables Leak" (arXiv:2512.08875) shows memorized digit-string reproduction and a no-box MIA that is "in some cases... even a perfect membership classifier on state-of-the-art models". "Risk In Context" (arXiv:2507.17066, KDD 2025 workshop) measures LLaMA 3.3 70B at up to 54 percentage points higher TPR@1%FPR than the safest baseline. Bordt et al. (COLM 2024) establish the verbatim memorisation underneath. This is a closed question — do not claim it.

Q3 — Would adding an LLM mechanism be scientifically defensible? AS A SYNTHESIS MECHANISM, NO, AND I RECOMMEND AGAINST IT. Four reasons, in descending force. (i) The field's own benchmark refuses to do it: Chen, Li, Gong, McKenna and Wang (SIGMOD 2025, arXiv:2504.14061) write verbatim "we don't consider some LLM-based methods. That is because LLMs are trained on extensive public datasets, which will introduce evaluation bias" — that is AIM's own author declining to benchmark LLM methods on validity grounds. (ii) SynthProof's primary dataset is contaminated: Bordt et al. Table 2 shows UCI Adult passes the header, feature-name, feature-value, feature-distribution and conditional-distribution memorization tests on both GPT-3.5 and GPT-4, while ACS Income fails every test. Any Adult result from an LLM mechanism would be uninterpretable. (iii) Tramèr, Kamath and Carlini (ICML 2024, arXiv:2212.06470) question "whether the use of large Web-scraped datasets should be viewed as differential-privacy-preserving" and warn it could "erode the public's trust in differential privacy as a meaningful definition of privacy" — a project selling a signed, defensible epsilon cannot rest it on a premise those three authors publicly contest. (iv) It is not even novel: two prior systems exist, and no published work shows an LLM mechanism beating AIM under DP (DP-2Stage benchmarks only against DP-GAN, DP-CTGAN and DP-VAE). Meanwhile Tab-PE (arXiv:2606.08259, Jun 2026) beats AIM by up to 10% and 28x faster — by explicitly ABANDONING foundation models for heuristic operators. The faculty asked for deep models; the honest answer is that a mechanism which voids the epsilon is worse than none, and the defensible version is to ship an LLM mechanism as a NEGATIVE CONTROL behind a flag — reuse the public DP-2Stage implementation, run Adult vs ACSIncome, and show the advantage evaporating for contamination reasons, mirroring the existing clique-selection result.

Q4 — DP-safe NLP on the schema/metadata side? PARTLY, but the mainstream version is NOT safe and this must not be assumed. SI-LLM (arXiv:2509.04632) infers schemas from "only column headers and cell values", randomly sampling 5 rows per table and up to 5 distinct values per column, with zero privacy discussion — sampling distinct column values is an un-noised domain release, the exact bug SynthProof already fixed in its profiler. The genuinely safe pattern is Hod, Rosenblatt & Stoyanovich (arXiv:2504.14368): generate from schema-level specs only, consuming zero budget — but note their honest negative result that it has LIMITED utility for tuning DP synthetic generators. On documentation, Croissant Baker (arXiv:2605.15079, May 2026) already does LLM Croissant generation but emits no privacy or epsilon fields and happily ingests record-level data; Giner-Miguelez et al. (arXiv:2404.15320) consume documentation text only and report 81.21% accuracy with GPT-3.5 hallucinating — an argument against LLM-authored fields in a document meant to be signed.

WHAT THIS AXIS DOES FOR THE SURVIVING RESULT. The best find is not an LLM mechanism at all. It is that UCI Adult and ACSIncome are separated by TWO independent confounds pointing the same direction, documented in two literatures that have not been connected: workload/clique-selection overlap (SynthProof's own result) and pretraining contamination (Bordt et al.). The SIGMOD 2025 benchmark knows about the second and disqualifies LLM methods for it, while scoring AIM and PrivMRF — which "dynamically select low-dimensional marginals" — on marginal-based metrics without applying the analogous standard. That asymmetry, stated against a verbatim quote from the field's own benchmark, is a stronger and better-armoured framing of the generalised claim than the claim currently has. The main threat to it is Tab-PE's opening sentence, which says SOTA methods "focus on minimizing low-order marginal query errors and overlook the challenges posed by high-order correlations" — close, but framed as a method deficiency rather than a measurement artefact. The claim survives in the narrowed form: not "these mechanisms are weak on high-order structure" (published) but "the benchmark score is decided by whether the scored statistic was one the mechanism selected" (not published in any source I fetched).

SATURATION. Reached for Q1-Q3: the final three search rounds returned predominantly already-seen sources (DP-2Stage, arXiv:2504.14061, DP-LLMTGen recurring). Q4 is thinner and I would not call it saturated — the metadata/documentation literature is large and I sampled it. Six fetch failures are recorded honestly in toolFailures; DP-Tabula (IEEE 11130155) could not be retrieved at all and nothing above depends on it. Yu et al. on DP fine-tuning of LMs was recalled but never fetched, so it is marked [UNVERIFIED] and excluded from the source table entirely. One follow-up worth doing before the literature chapter is finalised: "How to DP-fy Your Data" (arXiv:2512.03238, Ponomareva, Xu, McMahan, Kairouz, Rosenblatt et al.) was found at search-snippet depth only and is likely to state the practitioner consensus on these exact tradeoffs.

## Claims this axis KILLED

### Adding an LLM-based mechanism to SynthProof would be a novel contribution to DP tabular synthesis.

**Killed by:** DP-LLMTGen (arXiv:2406.01457, Jun 2024) and DP-2Stage (arXiv:2412.02467, TMLR March 2025) both already do DP-SGD fine-tuning of a pretrained LLM for tabular synthesis with a formal (eps,delta) guarantee by post-processing. DP-2Stage is peer-reviewed at eps=1 and eps=8, delta=1e-5, on Adult among others. A fifth mechanism in SynthProof would be a reimplementation, two years late, of published work.

`CONFIDENCE: high — both papers fetched, DP-2Stage read in full text with epsilons, datasets and baselines confirmed`

### There is no LLM-based DP tabular synthesizer with a formal end-to-end epsilon guarantee (Question 1, pessimistic framing).

**Killed by:** DP-2Stage explicitly states its guarantee: only Stage 2 touches private data, so 'assuming that the public dataset used is indeed public, the privacy guarantees of our method hold exactly'. DP-LLMTGen claims the same by post-processing. The formal answer to Q1 is YES. The interesting failure is not the absence of a guarantee but the conditions the guarantee is contingent on — see the surviving candidates.

`CONFIDENCE: high — verbatim from DP-2Stage full text`

### SynthProof could contribute the finding that LLM-based tabular synthesis leaks memorised training rows (Question 2 as a novelty claim).

**Killed by:** Fully occupied. 'When Tables Leak' (arXiv:2512.08875) shows LLM tabular generators reproduce memorized digit strings and that a no-box MIA needing only the synthetic output is 'in some cases... even a perfect membership classifier on state-of-the-art models'. 'Risk In Context' (arXiv:2507.17066, KDD 2025 workshop) measures LLaMA 3.3 70B at up to 54 percentage points higher TPR@1%FPR than the safest baseline. Bordt et al. (COLM 2024) establish the underlying verbatim memorisation.

`CONFIDENCE: high — three independent sources, two fetched at abstract depth with quantitative claims, one at full text`

### Using an empirical audit to catch DP violations in an LLM synthesizer caused by pretraining contamination would be novel.

**Killed by:** SynBench (arXiv:2509.14594) already does exactly this and reports it verbatim: MIA audits 'provide empirical evidence that observed privacy loss can exceed the bounds implied by claimed (eps,delta)-DP configurations' on contaminated datasets, because 'access to private data during pre-training is not accounted for in the privacy guarantees'. Note this also sits on top of the project's already-dead 'defect-finding by self-audit' and 'dual-sided assurance' claims — it is the same idea a third time.

`CONFIDENCE: high — verbatim quotes from full text`

### Using an LLM to auto-generate SynthProof's Privacy Data Sheet, Croissant record, or dataset documentation would be novel.

**Killed by:** Croissant Baker (arXiv:2605.15079, May 2026, 20 authors incl. Vanschoren, Ghassemi, Pollard) is LLM-driven automated Croissant metadata generation. Giner-Miguelez et al. (arXiv:2404.15320) do LLM-based enrichment of dataset documentation from paper text. CARDGEN/CARDBENCH cover model and data cards. This also re-treads the project's already-dead 'structured privacy labels' (Dibia et al. 2507.15997) and 'machine-checkable release artefacts' claims.

`CONFIDENCE: high — Croissant Baker abstract and author list fetched; Giner-Miguelez abstract fetched`

### Generating pseudo/surrogate public data from the schema so that structure-learning costs no privacy budget would be a novel SynthProof contribution.

**Killed by:** Hod, Rosenblatt & Stoyanovich (arXiv:2504.14368) generate surrogate public tabular data from schema-level specs alone using an LLM, consuming zero budget, and evaluate it on DP auxiliary tasks. DP-2Stage's Stage 1 is the same idea applied to LLM fine-tuning. Additionally the project's own 'budget-charged domain profiling' claim is already dead against Ganev et al.

`CONFIDENCE: high — arXiv abstract fetched directly`

### LLM-based schema/column-semantics inference is inherently DP-safe because it only touches metadata (Question 4, optimistic framing).

**Killed by:** FALSE as stated. SI-LLM (arXiv:2509.04632) uses 'only column headers and cell values' — it randomly samples 5 non-duplicate rows per table and up to 5 distinct values per column, and contains zero discussion of privacy. Sampling distinct column values IS an un-noised domain release. The DP-safe variant exists only if you restrict input to headers plus DP-released domains, which is what Hod et al. do and what SynthProof's profiler already does after its audit fix.

`CONFIDENCE: high — SI-LLM read at full text, sampling procedure quoted`

## Candidates that survived this axis

### UCI Adult and ACSIncome are separated by TWO independent confounds pointing the same direction, not one. SynthProof has already shown confound #1: AIM's clique selection decides its structure score (selected on Adult, selected at 1 of 3 epsilons on ACS, error tracking selection exactly: 0.0977 not-selected -> 0.0395 selected -> 0.0626 not-selected). Confound #2 is established independently by Bordt et al. (COLM 2024) Table 2: Adult is memorized by GPT-3.5/GPT-4 (passes header, feature-name, feature-value, feature-distribution and conditional-distribution tests) while ACS Income and ACS Travel fail every memorization test. So Adult flatters marginal-based mechanisms through workload overlap AND would flatter LLM-based mechanisms through pretraining contamination; ACSIncome does neither. The claim: a DP-synthesis benchmark anchored on UCI Adult is confounded twice over, and the two confounds have been documented in separate literatures that have not been connected.

- **Why it might survive:** Each half is published; the conjunction is not. Bordt et al. are a tabular-LLM-evaluation paper with no DP content. The DP-synthesis benchmark literature (Chen et al. SIGMOD 2025) knows about the contamination confound and excludes LLM methods because of it, but does not know about — or at least does not state — the selection confound for the marginal methods it does include. SynthProof is unusually well positioned to make the conjunction because it already ran both datasets across four mechanisms with the clique-selection diagnosis in hand, and because Adult+ACSIncome is exactly the memorized/not-memorized pair.
- **Nearest prior work:** Bordt et al., arXiv:2404.06209, COLM 2024 (contamination half, no DP). Chen et al., arXiv:2504.14061, SIGMOD 2025 (excludes LLMs for contamination bias, includes AIM without selection analysis). Sun et al., SynBench arXiv:2509.14594 (contamination breaking audited epsilon, text only, not tabular). No fetched source makes the conjunction.
- **Confidence:** medium — the two halves are solidly fetched and verified; the conjunction is my INFERENCE and I found no source stating it, but absence of evidence over 16 searches is weaker than a positive check
- **How to test it:** No new experiments needed for the argument itself. To strengthen: run Bordt et al.'s four memorization tests (header, row completion, feature completion, first token) on whatever base model a hypothetical LLM mechanism would use, against both Adult and ACSIncome, and publish the pass/fail table alongside the existing clique-selection table. Two tables, same two datasets, two different mechanisms of confounding — that is the figure. It costs API calls, not a new mechanism.

### The DP-synthesis benchmark literature applies an evaluation-validity standard asymmetrically: it disqualifies LLM-based mechanisms for having seen the evaluation data, but does not apply the analogous standard to workload-adaptive mechanisms that have effectively seen the evaluation statistic. Chen et al. (SIGMOD 2025) write verbatim 'we don't consider some LLM-based methods. That is because LLMs are trained on extensive public datasets, which will introduce evaluation bias' — while in the same paper scoring AIM and PrivMRF, which 'dynamically select low-dimensional marginals', on predominantly marginal-based fidelity metrics, and reporting them as the winners.

- **Why it might survive:** This is a critique of benchmark methodology grounded in a verbatim quote from the field's own most recent benchmark, co-authored by AIM's author. It is not a claim about a mechanism, so it is not killed by any mechanism paper. Du & Li (CCS 2025) independently criticise low-order statistic metrics but propose a different fix (Wasserstein) and never raise selection overlap. Tab-PE (June 2026) criticises the same metrics but as a method deficiency, not a measurement artefact.
- **Nearest prior work:** Chen et al., arXiv:2504.14061 (the target). Du & Li, arXiv:2402.06806, CCS 2025 (adjacent critique, different diagnosis). Tran et al., Tab-PE, arXiv:2606.08259 ('state-of-the-art methods often focus on minimizing low-order marginal query errors and overlook the challenges posed by high-order correlations' — closest published sentence, but about method capability not scoring validity).
- **Confidence:** medium — the Chen et al. quote is verbatim-verified and load-bearing; the 'does not discuss selection bias' half is a negative finding from one full-text read plus the fetcher's explicit statement, and would be safer after a targeted keyword pass over the full PDF
- **How to test it:** Grep the Chen et al. camera-ready PDF for 'bias', 'select', 'workload', 'overlap', 'fair comparison' to confirm the negative. Then, on SynthProof's own data, report AIM's error on selected vs not-selected pairs as a paired distribution rather than the single pair currently used — if the gap is large and consistent, the asymmetry argument is empirical, not rhetorical.

### An LLM mechanism belongs in SynthProof ONLY as a negative control / confound demonstration, never as a fifth synthesis mechanism. Concretely: ship it behind a flag, run it on Adult and ACSIncome, show that its Adult advantage vanishes on ACSIncome, and attribute the gap to contamination rather than fidelity — the same experimental shape as the existing clique-selection result.

- **Why it might survive:** It converts the faculty's 'deep ML/DL/NL models' requirement into evidence rather than into a broken epsilon. It requires no claim that the LLM mechanism is good, so it is not killed by DP-LLMTGen or DP-2Stage being prior art on the mechanism. And it is honest about the guarantee: the mechanism can be reported as [epsilon not certified — pretraining exposure unaccounted] in the Privacy Data Sheet, which is arguably a better demonstration of what the data sheet is FOR than a fifth clean mechanism would be.
- **Nearest prior work:** SynBench (arXiv:2509.14594) does the contamination-breaks-audited-epsilon experiment for TEXT generation with Llama-3 on nine datasets. No fetched source does it for TABULAR data on a memorized/not-memorized dataset pair. Bordt et al. supply the memorization ground truth but run no DP mechanism.
- **Confidence:** medium-low — defensible as a thesis chapter and as an answer to the faculty ask, but it is a second-order contribution and the SynBench precedent means the IDEA is not novel, only its tabular instantiation on this specific dataset pair
- **How to test it:** Use DP-2Stage's public implementation (github.com/tejuafonja/DP-2Stage) rather than writing a mechanism — it already runs on Adult at eps=1 and eps=8, delta=1e-5. Add ACSIncome. Compare against SynthProof's existing AIM and independent-marginals baselines at matched epsilon. Predict: DP-2Stage looks competitive on Adult, degrades on ACSIncome. Critically, run SynthProof's own auditor against it and check whether audited epsilon exceeds the proved epsilon on Adult but not on ACSIncome. That single plot would be the chapter.

### A DP-safe metadata-only NLP path exists and is narrow but real: restrict any LLM to (a) column HEADERS and free-text descriptions, and (b) DP-released domains from the already-budget-charged profiler — never raw cell values — and record that restriction as an attested, zero-budget, schema-only step in the ledger and Privacy Data Sheet. Croissant Baker emits no privacy or epsilon fields and ingests record-level data; SI-LLM samples raw cell values with zero privacy discussion. Neither declares what the LLM was allowed to see.

- **Why it might survive:** The surviving Croissant claim from the project's existing MVP already covers privacy/epsilon fields in a Croissant record. What is genuinely unoccupied here is narrower: an attested provenance statement about the LLM's INPUT SCOPE — a machine-checkable assertion that the documentation-generating model saw only headers and DP-released domains. No fetched source does this. It is the natural extension of the project's existing profiler-audit fix into the documentation layer.
- **Nearest prior work:** Croissant Baker, arXiv:2605.15079 (LLM Croissant generation, no privacy fields, ingests records). Giner-Miguelez et al., arXiv:2404.15320 (documentation-text-only input, so DP-safe by accident, but no privacy claim and 81% accuracy with hallucination). Hod et al., arXiv:2504.14368 (schema-only LLM use, zero budget — the closest, but for surrogate DATA not documentation). SI-LLM, arXiv:2509.04632 (the unsafe counterexample).
- **Confidence:** low — this is a small increment on a claim the project already banked, it is adjacent to the already-dead 'budget-charged domain profiling' and 'machine-checkable release artefacts' claims, and Giner-Miguelez's hallucination finding argues against LLM-authored content in a signed document at all. Report it as a design note, not as a novelty claim.
