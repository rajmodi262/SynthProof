01Act I — The Promise Broken

A row is a person. Deleting the name doesn't change that.

Turn on the "anonymous" fields one at a time and watch a crowd of 600 people collapse to a single individual.

Still anonymous 600 no fields applied
Identified 0 uniquely singled out

Sweeney (2000) showed that 87% of the US population is uniquely identified by ZIP + date of birth + sex alone. The Netflix Prize dataset was re-identified from public IMDb ratings (Narayanan & Shmatikov, 2008). AOL's "anonymised" search logs named users within days.

De-identification is not a privacy guarantee. It is a hope about what the attacker doesn't already know.
02Act I

So teams generate synthetic data instead. Generative models memorise.

A generator trained without a formal guarantee can — and does — emit training records verbatim. Watch the stream.

scanning output…
  • "Synthetic" describes how it was made, not what it reveals.
  • Outliers are memorised first — the rare, the sick, the wealthy. Exactly the people who need protection most.
  • Without a formal mechanism there is no upper bound at all on what a released row can disclose.
  • And today, almost nothing shipped as synthetic data arrives with any statement of how private it actually is.
This is the whole reason the project exists. Not to make synthetic data — plenty of tools do that — but to make synthetic data that says something true about itself.
03Act II — Two Half-Answers

Two research communities. Neither ships what a data owner needs.

Camp A — Formal DP

Proves an (ε, δ) bound. Composes. Peer-reviewed.

  • Worst-case. ε is often so loose it says little — released values of 10 or more are common.
  • Unverified. Nobody checks the code matches the proof. One wrong line silently voids it.
  • Partial. Schema discovery, clipping, and tuning routinely read the raw data for free.

Camp B — Empirical Auditing

Runs real attacks. Reports real numbers.

  • No guarantee. "This attack failed" is not "no attack succeeds."
  • No budget. Nothing accumulates. Ten releases compound leakage silently.
  • No provenance. Results live in a paper appendix, not attached to the artefact.

Mechanism: 14×14 histogram, Gaussian mechanism at δ=1e-5, σ = √(2·ln(1.25/δ))/ε, counts clamped at zero and renormalised — the same shape as our AIM-style marginal baseline. Live measurements of a toy mechanism — not results from our paper.

06Act III

Differential privacy composes — but only if someone is counting.

Every spend is a block, chained by real SHA-256 computed in your browser. Try to rewrite history.

Chain intact — 4 blocks verified

Append-only

Each block commits to its predecessor's hash. Editing any past spend invalidates every block after it — which is exactly what you just saw.

Signed

Ed25519 over the canonical bytes of each entry, so a third party with the public key can verify the organisation's spend history independently.

Organisational

Nothing in current practice stops a second team re-releasing the same table and doubling the real privacy loss. The ledger is what stops it.

08Act IV — The Plan

Four contributions.

Each ingredient exists somewhere in the literature. None of them ship together — the composition is where the contribution lives.

01 · Dual-sided assurance

εproved and εaudited, same run, same artefact, reported as a pair.

DP papers report the bound. Auditing papers report attacks. A released dataset gets neither. We make the ratio a published metric.

02 · Budget-charged profiling

Schema discovery and range estimation are charged like any other query.

Most pipelines silently read min/max and category domains off the raw data — an unaccounted leak that invalidates the headline ε. We priced it.

03 · Append-only signed ledger

Ed25519 over SHA-256, tracking organisational spend across releases.

DP composes only if someone counts. Nothing today stops the same table being released twice at full budget.

04 · Subgroup privacy disparity

Does a uniform ε protect minority records as well as majority ones?

Our strongest empirical question and the most socially consequential. DP's guarantee is worst-case per record — measured leakage need not be uniform.

09Act IV

Three preregistered hypotheses, and what we build them on.

H1 · The gap

εaudited/εproved differs systematically across mechanism families at equal target ε.

Test: AIM vs copula vs independent marginals, ε ∈ {0.5,1,2,4,8}, 5 seeds, bootstrapped CIs.

H2 · Disparate impact

Under uniform allocation, minority subgroups show higher measured leakage.

Test: per-subgroup canary audits on ACS PUMS, stratified by race and sex.

H3 · Smarter allocation

Utility-weighted allocation beats uniform at equal total spend.

Test: ledger-driven allocator vs uniform, downstream macro-F1 at fixed ε.

11Act IV

Who needs a dataset that argues for itself?

Hospitals & health research

A registry sharing outcomes with a university today faces an 18-month DPA negotiation. With a data sheet, the ethics board reviews a number, not a promise — and the ledger proves the cohort wasn't released five times over.

Banks & fintech

Fraud models need data that never leaves the bank. Certified synthetic replacements let vendors and model-risk teams work on realistic data with an auditable exposure figure.

Government & statistics

The US Census already ships DP microdata. What it doesn't ship is an empirical audit alongside the bound. Our format is a candidate template for any agency releasing under a formal guarantee.

ML teams & MLOps

Training-data provenance is becoming a compliance artefact under the EU AI Act. A signed privacy certificate on the training set is exactly what a model card should cite.

Reproducible research

Publish the synthetic companion to your paper's dataset with a verifiable privacy statement, so reviewers can rerun your analysis without an IRB.

A benchmark for the DP community

The proved-vs-audited sweep is itself a contribution: a public table of how tight each mechanism's bound actually is, on real data, with confidence intervals.

12The Ask

What could go wrong — and what we need from you.

Risk · The audit finds nothing

A correct DP mechanism may leak too little to detect, leaving εaudited ≈ 0. Mitigation: scale canary count until the bound is informative, report the detection floor, and validate the auditor on a deliberately broken mechanism so a null is credible rather than vacuous.

Risk · We reimplement theory badly

Hand-rolled DP math is how privacy claims become false. We already made this mistake once. Mitigation: delegate composition to dp_accounting, differential-test against autodp in CI, never write a bound we can't cite.

Risk · Scope too large

Four mechanisms × four attacks × three datasets × five seeds is a lot of compute. Mitigation: every milestone is independently demoable. If M3 slips, M2 is still a complete project.

Risk · Utility collapses

At small ε the data may be useless, making the frontier uninteresting. Mitigation: that is itself the finding. A frontier showing where usable utility ends is what regulators most need.

Scope sign-off

Is M0–M2 the right capstone boundary, with M3 as stretch? We'd rather cut early than over-promise.

Direction on H2

Subgroup privacy disparity is our most publishable angle. Worth pulling ahead of H3?

Compute & data

LiRA needs many shadow models. Guidance on lab compute, and on any real dataset we could responsibly use.
